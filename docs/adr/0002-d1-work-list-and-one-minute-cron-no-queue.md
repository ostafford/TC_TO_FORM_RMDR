# A D1 work list driven by a one-minute cron, no Queue

The integration playbook (`../CLAUDE.md`) gives a write-heavy Worker a Queue consumer from day one. We don't. Every step here waits on a clock (the end of the Grace Period, the escalation delay, a Scheduled Shift's planned end, the polling sweep), so one Worker on the Workers Paid plan keeps a D1 table of Shifts, each with the time its next step is due, and a cron every minute does whatever is due. The table already is the queue: an unfinished row is picked up by the next run, backoff means moving its due time, and an edit, a deletion or a late submission just updates the row. That can't be done to a delayed Queue message, which can't be cancelled.

## Considered Options

- **Queue messages with `delaySeconds`**: they can't be cancelled when a Shift is edited or a form arrives, and the polling sweep would still need a cron. That's two schedulers.
- **A Durable Object alarm per Shift**: precise and cancellable, but it's a second storage system and still needs the cron.

## Consequences

- The playbook's two reasons for a Queue are covered another way. The 50-call cap is gone on the Paid plan. Retry safety comes from each cron run **leasing** the rows it takes, so overlapping runs never work the same Shift, and from **claiming every send in D1 before it goes out** (marked done on `ok`, released on a send that answered with a failure, kept on one that got no answer: see the next point).
- Messages to people (the Reminder, the Escalation and Escalation Summary, the tick DM) are at most once, and so is the Reminder Task create, which isn't a message but would make a second task. If the Worker dies between a claim and its send, or the send gets no answer (a timeout or a dropped connection), the message may have gone out, so its claim is kept, not released: it is never sent again. A claim stuck for more than 10 minutes is given up on as unconfirmed and listed in the next Failure Alert, so a lost message still reaches a human. A send that gets an answer saying it failed (a 429, a 5xx) is released and retried. Being nagged twice is worse than a rare message followed up by hand from the alert (#75).
- Failure Alerts and all clears are the exception: at least once. One that gets no answer is released and posted again on the next run, because a stuck alert has nothing left to report it, and a duplicate in the Alerts Channel costs little. (A run that dies mid-post still leaves its claim to be settled as posted, so the alert's 24-hour hold isn't reset.)
- Each run stays polite to the account's shared rate limit: oldest due first, and it stops for the minute once less than 20% of the limit remains, or on a 429. A loop stops partway only where the rest can wait without counting as a failure: an Escalation's or Summary's recipients not yet sent to wait for the next run. A step's paged reads and scheduler lookups stop only on the 429 itself; stopping them on a low budget would fail the step and start its backoff, so with less than 20% left they finish (#95).
